Which diabetic patients come back to hospital within 30 days?

An analysis of 101,766 real hospital stays of diabetic patients in 130 US hospitals (1999–2008), from the UCI Machine Learning Repository. It covers data quality, readmission drivers, a risk model the care team could use at discharge, and a re-test of a result from the original research paper.

Risk model

Can we flag high-risk patients at discharge?

A logistic regression on 20 variables that are known at discharge, trained on 75% of patients and tested on the other 25%. I compared it with a simple score adapted from the LACE index (length of stay, acuity, comorbidity, emergency visits), which clinics already use.

Model vs simple rules

MethodAUCReadmissions caught (top 20%)

AUC 0.5 = random guessing, 1.0 = perfect. Published models on this dataset usually reach 0.60–0.70. Readmission depends on many things that happen after discharge and aren't in hospital records.

Recommendation: use the model to pick the top 20% of patients for a follow-up call or early clinic visit. The simple LACE-style score is weaker here, because this data has no full comorbidity index.

Re-testing a published finding

Is HbA1c testing linked to fewer readmissions?

The original paper (Strack et al., 2014) reported that measuring HbA1c was linked to lower readmission, and that the effect depends on the main diagnosis. I re-tested this on the cleaned cohort with logistic regression, adjusting for age, length of stay, prior visits, number of diagnoses, admission source and discharge destination.

Method

How it is built

  1. Data: the UCI "Diabetes 130-US Hospitals 1999–2008" dataset: 101,766 encounters, 50 columns, plus 3 lookup tables for admission type, admission source and discharge destination.
  2. SQL (DuckDB): 00_data_quality_checks → 01_staging (joins, ICD-9 diagnosis groups, cohort) → 02_readmission_profile → 03_risk_score → 04_hba1c_analysis.
  3. Python: scikit-learn logistic regression with a train/test split; statsmodels for adjusted odds ratios, confidence intervals and an interaction test.
  4. Limits: US data from 1999–2008; readmissions to other hospitals are not recorded; the HbA1c result is an association, not a causal effect.
Data: Strack B. et al. (2014), "Impact of HbA1c Measurement on Hospital Readmission Rates", BioMed Research International. UCI Machine Learning Repository, CC BY 4.0. De-identified data.Sonam Rohilla · Source code and SQL
